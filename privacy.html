The short version

  • We collect the things you tell Ormy, so it can remind you about them. That is the whole point of the app.
  • Your messages go to Anthropic to generate replies. They are not used to train AI models.
  • We never sell your data, never show ads, and never share it with advertisers or data brokers.

  • If you pin a reminder to a place, we store that place and your phone watches for it. Ormy never keeps a history of where you have been.
  • Settings → Delete account permanently erases everything. It is one tap, and we do not ask why.

2.1 What we do not collect

  • Your device calendar. Ormy keeps its own internal calendar and does not read Apple Calendar or Google Calendar.
  • Your contacts, photos or files.
  • A history of your movements. Location reminders work by asking iOS to watch a circle you drew and to wake Ormy when you cross its edge. Your phone does the watching. We are told that a crossing happened, not where you were before it, and we never build a trail of your movements. See section 2.2 for what we do store.
  • Voice recordings. Where you dictate to Ormy, speech-to-text is performed by your device's own operating system. We receive the resulting text, never the audio.
  • Payment card details. Purchases are handled by Apple. We never see, hold or process your card.
  • Advertising or cross-app tracking identifiers. Ormy contains no advertising SDKs and does not track you across other apps or websites.

2.2 Location reminders, in detail

This is the part of Ormy that has the most to explain, so it gets its own section.

A location reminder is one you attach to a place instead of a time — "get milk when I reach the shop". To make that work, three things happen:

  • You choose the place. You either search for an address or use where you are standing. We store the coordinates of the spot you picked, the radius you chose, and a label for it. That label is usually the street address, which your phone works out for us.
  • Your phone watches for it. We hand those circles to iOS, which monitors them at the operating-system level, including when Ormy is closed. This is why the app asks for "Always" location permission. iOS wakes the app when you cross an edge; it does not report your position to us the rest of the time, and we do not ask it to.
  • While the app is open, and only then, Ormy reads your current position once so it can show you how far away each place is. That reading is used on your device and is not sent to our servers or stored.

What this means in plain terms. We hold a list of the places you chose to tell us about. We do not hold a record of where you have been, when you were there, or where you are now. Nobody can query our database to find out where you are, because that is not in it.

You can remove a place at any time by editing or deleting the reminder, and you can switch location permission off in your device settings — the rest of the app carries on working, and only location reminders stop firing.

3. Health and other sensitive information

Ormy is designed for people who need reminders for things like medication, therapy sessions, and hospital appointments. If you put those into Ormy, you are giving us information about your health.

Under data protection law that is "special category" data, and it needs a stronger legal basis than the rest. Ours is your explicit consent, which you give by choosing to enter it.

In practice, this covers:

  • a reminder whose title reveals a health condition, treatment or medication;
  • anything you tell Ormy in conversation about your physical or mental health;
  • onboarding answers about the difficulties you experience, where those relate to a condition or disability;
  • a place you pin, where the place itself gives it away — a clinic, a hospital, a pharmacy or a treatment centre.

We use that information for one purpose only: running Ormy for you. We do not profile you on the basis of it, we do not share it with anyone beyond the providers listed in section 5, and we never use it for marketing.

You can withdraw that consent at any time by deleting the reminder or message concerned, or by deleting your account. Withdrawing consent does not affect anything we lawfully did before you withdrew it. If you would rather not give us health information at all, you can keep the wording of your reminders vague — Ormy will remind you about "appointment, 3pm" just as reliably as it will remind you about anything more specific.

Please also avoid putting other people's sensitive information into Ormy. A birthday reminder for a friend is fine. Details of someone else's medical treatment are not yours to share with us.

4. How your data is used to send you notifications

This is the one processing activity worth spelling out, because it runs while you are not using the app.

There are two of these. The first is geofencing, described in section 2.2: your phone, not our server, watches for the places you pinned, and does so while the app is closed. When it wakes Ormy, the alert is prepared on your device.

The second is scheduling. Our servers check, on a schedule, which reminders are due and which devices are at the local time you asked to be checked in at. When one matches, a notification is sent to your device through Apple's Push Notification service, by way of Expo's push service. The notification contains the reminder text you wrote, which means it may appear on your lock screen. If you would rather that text were not visible to anyone who picks up your phone, you can hide notification previews in your device settings.

None of this involves a decision about you that produces legal effects or anything similarly significant. Ormy has no automated decision-making of that kind, and does not profile you for advertising or credit purposes.

7. How long we keep things

The retention period for each category is in the table in section 2. In general:

  • While your account exists, we keep what the app needs to work.
  • The activity log is trimmed after 90 days. Individual entries are deleted; only non-itemised running totals remain, so your all-time figures do not reset.
  • When you delete your account, your user record, reminders, messages, check-ins, activity log, recaps and push tokens are permanently deleted from our database. Your authentication record with Clerk is deleted at the same time. Feedback you sent is stripped of your name, email address and account identifier.
  • Backups. Deleted data may persist in encrypted infrastructure backups for a short rolling period before those backups expire. It is not restored to the live service and is not accessible to us in the ordinary course.
  • Purchase records. Apple keeps its own records of transactions for its own legal and tax purposes. We cannot delete those, and neither can you.

8. How we protect your data

  • Encryption in transit — all traffic between the app, our servers and every provider uses TLS.
  • Encryption at rest — our database provider encrypts stored data.
  • Access control on every query — every server function that reads or writes your data first establishes who is asking, and returns only that person's records. Users cannot reach each other's data.
  • Managed authentication — we do not build or store passwords ourselves. That is handled by Clerk, which specialises in it.
  • No payment data — we never hold card details, so they cannot be taken from us.
  • Least privilege — access to production systems is limited to those who need it, protected by multi-factor authentication.
  • Rate and spend limits — to contain abuse and runaway automated usage.

No system is perfectly secure, and we will not pretend otherwise. If a breach happens that is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, we will notify the relevant supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and we will tell you directly and without undue delay where the risk to you is high.

9. Your rights

If you are in the UK, the EEA, or another region with comparable law, you have the following rights over your personal data. They are free to exercise.

  • Access — get a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectification — have inaccurate data corrected.
  • Erasure — have your data deleted. You can do this yourself, immediately, in Settings → Delete account.
  • Portability — receive your data in a structured, machine-readable format, or have it sent to another provider where technically feasible.
  • Restriction — have us pause processing while a dispute about accuracy or lawfulness is resolved.
  • Objection — object to processing we carry out on the basis of legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent — at any time, for anything we rely on consent for, including health-related content and notifications.
  • Complain — to a data protection authority (see section 10).

9.1 How to exercise them

To delete everything, use Settings → Delete account in the app. It is immediate and needs nothing from us.

For anything else, email kyle.orm@turbolt.co.uk from the address on your account, telling us which right you are exercising. We will respond within one month. If your request is genuinely complex we may extend that by up to two further months, and we will tell you why within the first month. We may ask you to confirm your identity before acting — only to make sure we are not handing your data to somebody else.
